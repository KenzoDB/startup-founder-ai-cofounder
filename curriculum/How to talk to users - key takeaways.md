Do not introduce your product idea during customer interviews—your role is to listen and understand the problem, not pitch a solution.
Six core interview questions: (1) Tell me how you do X today, (2) What is the hardest part about doing X, (3) Why is it hard, (4) How often do you have to do X, (5) Why is it important for your company to do X, (6) What do you currently do to solve this problem.
Questions to avoid: 'Will you use our product?' (they'll say yes meaninglessly), 'Which features would make product X better?' (your job, not theirs), yes/no questions, 'How would a better product look?' (they aren't product designers), and asking two questions at once.
Users generally surface good problems but propose bad solutions—e.g., Gmail users asked to see inbox and email on the same screen when the real problem was slow load times; Airbnb guests asked for host phone numbers when the real problem was lack of trust.
A 5-minute video interview is more valuable than 500 or 5,000 survey responses.
To evaluate if a problem is valuable: (1) Are people already paying for other solutions? (2) Do people have existing solutions they're happy with (e.g., Excel/Google Sheets is a formidable competitor—you must be dramatically better)? (3) How easy is it to sell to this audience (e.g., plumbers/contractors rarely change tools vs. startups who try new things readily)?
Use follow-up prompts like 'Tell me more about that,' 'What do you mean by that?' and 'Why is that important to you?' to get deeper answers.
When testing a prototype, don't tell users what to do—give them a goal (e.g., 'try to make a booking') and watch them navigate; have them speak their thoughts aloud as they go.
Find early users through LinkedIn searches (by relevant title keywords), Reddit forums, Slack/Discord communities, coworkers, and in-person events.
After interviews, organize notes into buckets of problems, identify the most important one, form a hypothesis for your MVP, and test it with the same users.
Create a Slack or WhatsApp group for early users to make them feel like exclusive insiders, show them product progress, and ship responses to their feedback quickly to build trust.
