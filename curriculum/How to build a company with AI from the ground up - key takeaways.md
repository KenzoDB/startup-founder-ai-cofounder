Build your company as a closed loop, not an open loop: every important process should capture information, feed it into an AI system, and use it to self-improve. Open loops (decide → execute without measuring) are inherently lossy.
Make your entire company queryable by AI: record meetings with AI note-takers, minimize DMs/emails, embed agents in communication channels, and build dashboards covering revenue, sales, engineering, hiring, and ops so AI has full context.
AI software factories are the evolution of TDD: humans write specs and tests that define success, AI agents generate code and iterate until tests pass. Some companies already have repos with zero handwritten code, only specs and test harnesses.
The three employee archetypes for an AI-native company (per Jack Dorsey's model): (1) IC/builder-operator who directly makes things (not just engineers — everyone builds), (2) DRI (directly responsible individual) focused on strategy and customer outcomes, (3) AI founder type who builds, coaches, and leads by example.
Maximize token usage, not headcount ('token maxing'). Run an uncomfortably high API bill because it replaces what would have been far more expensive headcount. One person with AI tools can equal what used to take a large engineering team.
Eliminate human middleware: if your company is queryable and artifact-rich, you should have almost no middle managers or coordinators. Company velocity equals information flow speed; every layer of human routing you remove is a direct speed gain.
Startups have a structural advantage over incumbents in going AI-native: no legacy systems, entrenched org charts, or thousands of people to retrain. Design systems, workflows, and culture around AI from day one.
Founders must personally develop conviction in AI tools by sitting with coding agents — do not delegate your AI strategy. Come to meetings with working prototypes, not pitch decks.
For sprint planning, give an agent access to Linear tickets, Slack channels, customer feedback, Notion docs, sales call recordings, and standups — it can analyze what shipped, how well it met customer needs, and propose more accurate future sprint plans. Teams doing this have cut sprint time in half and achieved close to 10x throughput.
