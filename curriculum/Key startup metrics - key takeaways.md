Track only 4-5 key metrics before launch—not 30 or 50. This number grows over time. Use simple tools like SQL queries or PostHog.
The three metrics that must appear at the top of every investor update are: revenue, burn rate (monthly costs minus revenue), and runway (cash divided by burn rate).
Agree on clear, written definitions of each metric with your whole team and keep them consistent over time—even if the definition isn't perfect. Changing definitions to make numbers look better only fools yourself.
Revenue is the best core metric for most B2B companies. Vanity metrics like GMV or gross transaction value can mask flat or declining revenue and lead founders to optimize for the wrong thing.
Retention is best visualized by stacking monthly cohorts: if cohorts stay fat (high retention), you build a growing 'layer cake' of revenue; if they decay to zero, you're endlessly filling a leaky bucket and will plateau.
Net dollar retention (NDR) = revenue from a cohort after 12 months ÷ original revenue. Early-stage B2B SaaS should target 125-150% NDR; mature companies 110-120%. NDR below 100% means something is wrong—fix churn before investing in top-of-funnel growth.
Three reasons early-stage NDR should be well above 100%: (1) you've likely underpriced initially, (2) you're adding features that justify higher prices, (3) you're getting better at sales and upselling.
For AI companies, API costs paid to OpenAI/Anthropic are real COGS even if covered by free credits—don't pretend gross margin is 95% when credits will expire.
Don't scale with negative unit economics. Fix them first, then grow. The blitz-scaling approach (subsidizing users to reach network effects) requires enormous capital and has left a 'wasteland' of failed startups.
For operationally intensive businesses (delivery, painting, installation), consider whether a software-only version could be sold to other operators at much higher gross margins.
Don't over-rely on metrics at small scale—you can't meaningfully A/B test button colors with a few hundred users. Reserve split tests for high-impact decisions like pricing ($80/year vs $200/year).
